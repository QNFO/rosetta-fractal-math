## 0. The Problem

We defined categories \(\mathcal{P}\) (Physical Dynamics) and \(\mathcal{C}\) (Computational Logic) and argued the translation functor \(F: \mathcal{P} \to \mathcal{C}\) is non-exact. But this framing smuggles in an assumption we have not examined: **Who chose \(\mathcal{C}\)? Why binary logic? Why bits?**

The answer is archaeological, not physical. Binary logic was chosen because:
1. Human cognition evolved for discrete counting (fingers, tokens)
2. Boolean algebra maps neatly to electronic switches (von Neumann architecture)
3. Error correction requires a discrete syndrome (threshold theorems)

**None of these are physical necessities. They are observer constraints.**

## 1. The Observer Constraint Algebra \(\mathcal{O}\)

### 1.1 Definition

Let \(\mathcal{H}_{\text{full}}\) be the full Hilbert space of the physical system (for the transmon: infinite-dimensional bosonic Fock space). Let \(\mathcal{O}\) be the **Observer Constraint Algebra** — the maximal algebra of observables that a specific class of observers (humans with binary cognition) can simultaneously measure and process.

Formally, \(\mathcal{O}\) is a subalgebra of \(\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{\text{full}})\) satisfying:
- **Discreteness:** \(\mathcal{O}\) is generated by a finite set of orthogonal projectors \(\{P_0, P_1, ..., P_{d-1}\}\) — the "computational basis"
- **Locality:** Each projector acts on a tensor factor of the total system (qubit-wise measurement)
- **Repeatability:** The measurement outcomes are classically readable (0/1 bits)

The projection \(\Pi_{\mathcal{O}}: \mathcal{H}_{\text{full}} \to \mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{O}} = \text{span}\{|0\rangle, |1\rangle\}^{\otimes n}\) is the **observer filter** — it throws away everything the observer cannot perceive.

### 1.2 The Observer Entropy \(S_{\text{Obs}}\)

The information discarded by the observer filter:

\[
S_{\text{Obs}} = -\text{Tr}\left[\rho_{\text{full}} \ln\left(\frac{\rho_{\text{full}}}{\Pi_{\mathcal{O}} \rho_{\text{full}} \Pi_{\mathcal{O}}^{\dagger}}\right)\right]
\]

This is the Kullback-Leibler divergence between the full density matrix and its projection onto the observer's accessible subspace. For the transmon:

\[
S_{\text{Obs}} \approx \ln\left(\frac{\dim(\mathcal{H}_{\text{full}})}{\dim(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{O}})}\right) \approx \ln\left(\frac{\infty}{2}\right) \to \infty
\]

Finite truncation to 12 accessible levels: \(S_{\text{Obs}} = \ln(12/2) \approx 1.79\) nats per qubit projection.

## 2. The Observer Topos \(\mathcal{T}\)

### 2.1 Motivation

The functor \(F: \mathcal{P} \to \mathcal{C}\) implicitly assumes \(\mathcal{C}\) is **the** category of computation. But \(\mathcal{C}\) is actually **an** category of computation — the one accessible to binary-cognitive observers.

A topos-theoretic framework makes this explicit.

### 2.2 Definition

Define the **Observer Topos** \(\mathcal{T} = \mathbf{Sh}(\mathcal{X}, J)\) where:
- \(\mathcal{X}\) is the **site** — the category of "measurement contexts" (commutative subalgebras of observables)
- \(J\) is the **Grothendieck topology** — specifies which families of measurement contexts "cover" a physical state
- For a classical binary observer: \(\mathcal{X}\) is the poset of Boolean subalgebras of \(\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{O}})\)
- For a continuous/Platonic observer: \(\mathcal{X}\) is the poset of ALL commutative subalgebras of \(\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{\text{full}})\)

### 2.3 The Geometric Morphism of Observation

The observer's cognitive filter corresponds to a **geometric morphism** between topoi:

\[
\phi: \mathcal{T}_{\text{Observer}} \longrightarrow \mathcal{T}_{\text{Physical}}
\]

with direct image \(\phi_*\) (the "forgetful" functor — maps the observer's coarse-grained reality to the physical fine-grained reality) and inverse image \(\phi^*\) (the "reconstruction" functor — maps physical states to observer-accessible measurements).

**The translation entropy \(S_{\text{Obs}}\) is the information loss of \(\phi^*\).**

\[

\[
