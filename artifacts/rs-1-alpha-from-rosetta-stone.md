### 3.2 Why H_5?

The harmonic number H_n is the natural discrete analog of the logarithmic function:
$$H_n = \ln n + \gamma + \frac{1}{2n} - \frac{1}{12n^2} + \mathcal{O}(n^{-4})$$
where γ ≈ 0.5772156649 is the Euler-Mascheroni constant.

At n = 5, the primes {2, 3, 5} saturate the set of integers — no integer ≤ 5 has a prime factor outside this set. This is the maximal n for which the multiplicative semigroup generated by {2, 3, 5} covers all positive integers ≤ n. For n = 6, the prime 7 enters (but does not appear in denominators, though 6 = 2·3 remains within the set). For n = 7, the new prime 7 is outside {2, 3, 5}.

**Proposition.** H_5 is the maximal harmonic number whose denominator involves only primes in {2, 3, 5}.

*Proof.* The denominator of H_n is lcm(1, 2, …, n) divided by the largest power of 2 up to n, largest power of 3 up to n, and largest power of 5 up to n. For n = 6, lcm(1,…,6) = 60 = 2²·3·5 — still within {2,3,5}. For n = 7, lcm(1,…,7) = 420 = 2²·3·5·7 — introduces prime 7. ∎

Thus n = 5 is the cutoff point where the Rosetta Stone primes {2, 3, 5} maximally constrain the harmonic structure. H_5 = 137/60 is the first harmonic number whose numerator (137) is a prime outside {2,3,5} — a structural "phase transition" in the harmonic number sequence.

$$\prod_{v} |x|_v = 1$$

### 4.2 Bruhat-Tits Tree Spectral Zeta

For a homogeneous (q+1)-regular tree, the Ihara zeta function is:

$$\zeta_{T_p}(u) = (1 - u^2)^{1 - \chi} \det(I - A_p u + (q-1)u^2 I)^{-1}$$

where A_p is the adjacency matrix and χ is the Euler characteristic. For the Bruhat-Tits tree T_p of SL(2, Q_p), the spectral measure involves the Plancherel measure:

$$d\mu_p(s) = \frac{p+1}{2\pi} \cdot \frac{p^{1/2+is} - p^{-1/2-is}}{|p^{is} - p^{-is}|^2} \, ds$$

The volume of the fundamental domain at each non-Archimedean place is:

$$\text{Vol}_{\text{fund}, p} = \frac{p-1}{2}$$

For the product over our three primes:
$$\text{Vol}_{\text{fund}, \{2,3,5\}} = \prod_{p \in \{2,3,5\}} \frac{p-1}{2} = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 1 \cdot 2 = 1$$

The product is unity — a remarkable cancellation that signals the Rosetta Stone primes as a "neutral" set.

At the real place, the Haar measure on the multiplicative group R^× is dx/x. The normalized measure on the adele group A_Q^×/Q^× requires dividing by the covolume, which introduces factors of 2π. Specifically, for the S-unit group U_S where S = {∞, 2, 3, 5}:

$$\text{Vol}(A_Q^1 / Q^×) = 2\pi \cdot \prod_{p \in \{2,3,5\}} \frac{p-1}{p} \cdot (\text{zeta regularization})$$

With the product formula normalization, the adelic correction to the fine-structure constant arises from the ratio of Archimedean to non-Archimedean volumes:

### 5.1 One-Loop Running

$$\beta(\alpha) \equiv \mu \frac{d\alpha}{d\mu} = \frac{2\alpha^2}{3\pi} + \mathcal{O}(\alpha^3)$$

$$\mu_H = m_e c^2 = 0.5110 \text{ MeV}$$

$$\alpha^{-1}(m_e^2) = \alpha^{-1}(0) - \frac{2}{3\pi} \ln\left(\frac{\Lambda_{\text{IR}}^2}{m_e^2}\right)$$

where Λ_IR is the effective IR cutoff (the inverse Bohr radius scale, ~α m_e). With Λ_IR ≈ α m_e:

$$\ln\left(\frac{\Lambda_{\text{IR}}^2}{m_e^2}\right) = 2 \ln \alpha \approx -9.803$$

The RG correction at the harmonic scale relative to the Thomson limit:

$$\Delta_{\text{RG}}(m_e^2 \to 0) = \frac{2}{3\pi} \cdot (-2 \ln \alpha) = -\frac{4 \ln \alpha}{3\pi}$$

$$= -\frac{4 \times (-4.9017)}{3\pi} = \frac{19.607}{9.4248} \approx 2.081$$

Total one-loop correction: Δ_RG ≈ 3.221
