## Abstract

We formalize the translation between continuous physical dynamics and discrete digital computation as a functor \(F: \mathcal{P} \to \mathcal{C}\) from the category \(\mathcal{P}\) of symplectic phase spaces under Hamiltonian flow to the category \(\mathcal{C}\) of finite-dimensional gate circuits. We prove that \(F\) is **non-exact**: the symplectic capacity (Gromov invariant) — a measure of phase-space volume preserved by Hamiltonian dynamics — has no image in \(\mathcal{C}\), where finite-dimensional Boolean projections collapse the symplectic structure. This non-exactness formalizes the map-territory confusion at the heart of digital quantum computing: forcing a continuous bosonic system into a binary gate scaffold incurs irreducible information loss. The result extends de Gosson's (2012) "Angel of Geometry vs. Demon of Algebra" framing into an explicit categorical proof.

## 1. Introduction: The Map-Territory Problem

The quantum computing industry has absorbed ~$35 billion while producing zero commercially viable machines (QNFO, 2026). We argue this failure is not primarily an engineering problem but a **category error**: the qubit-gate-circuit model projects a particle ontology onto a continuous, field-theoretic reality.

This paper formalizes the mismatch. We define two categories — \(\mathcal{P}\) (physical dynamics) and \(\mathcal{C}\) (computational logic) — and a translation functor \(F: \mathcal{P} \to \mathcal{C}\). We then prove that \(F\) cannot be exact using Gromov's non-squeezing theorem, which states that the symplectic area of a 2-dimensional cross-section of phase space is bounded below by a topological invariant with no analog in any finite discrete category.

## 2. Category \(\mathcal{P}\): Physical Dynamics

### 2.1 Objects

\(\mathcal{P}\) has as objects **symplectic manifolds** \((M^{2n}, \omega)\) where:
- \(M\) is a \(2n\)-dimensional smooth manifold (the classical phase space)
- \(\omega = \sum_{i=1}^{n} dp_i \wedge dq_i\) is a closed, non-degenerate 2-form
- For quantum systems: \(M\) is replaced by the projective Hilbert space \(\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{H})\) with the Fubini-Study symplectic form

### 2.2 Morphisms

Morphisms in \(\mathcal{P}\) are **Hamiltonian symplectomorphisms** \(\varphi_t^H: M \to M\) generated by a Hamiltonian function \(H: M \to \mathbb{R}\) via Hamilton's equations:

\[
\dot{q}_i = \frac{\partial H}{\partial p_i}, \quad \dot{p}_i = -\frac{\partial H}{\partial q_i}
\]

Equivalently: \(\iota_{X_H}\omega = dH\) where \(X_H\) is the Hamiltonian vector field. The flow \(\varphi_t^H\) preserves the symplectic form: \((\varphi_t^H)^*\omega = \omega\).

For the quantum case, morphisms are unitary operators \(U(t) = e^{-iHt/\hbar}\) acting on \(\mathcal{H}\), which preserve transition probabilities: \(|\langle \psi|U^\dagger U|\phi\rangle| = |\langle\psi|\phi\rangle|\).

### 2.3 The Transmon's Object in \(\mathcal{P}\)

For the specific case of the superconducting transmon (\(E_J/E_C = 50\), \(\alpha_r = 1.9\%\)):

- The phase space is the cylinder \(M = S^1 \times \mathbb{R}\) with coordinates \((\phi, n)\)
- The symplectic form is \(\omega = d\phi \wedge dn\)
- The Hamiltonian is \(H = 4E_C n^2 - E_J\cos(\phi)\)
- The phase \(\phi\) is \(S^1\)-valued (continuous angular coordinate)
- The charge \(n\) has discrete spectrum but the underlying manifold is continuous

**Key invariant:** The symplectic area of any 2-dimensional cross-section. For the transmon, the "area" between neighboring energy levels is \(\hbar\) — the Heisenberg uncertainty volume.

## 3. Category \(\mathcal{C}\): Computational Logic

### 3.1 Objects

\(\mathcal{C}\) has as objects **finite-dimensional computational state spaces**:
- For \(n\) qubits: \(\mathcal{H}_2^{\otimes n}\), the \(2^n\)-dimensional complex Hilbert space with a distinguished computational basis \(\{|b_1 b_2 ... b_n\rangle\}_{b_i \in \{0,1\}}\)
- For classical bits: Boolean lattices \(\mathbb{B}^n = \{0,1\}^n\) with the natural partial order \(x \leq y \iff x_i \leq y_i \; \forall i\)

**Crucially:** Objects in \(\mathcal{C}\) are **finite and discrete**. There is no continuous coordinate — "between" \(|0\rangle\) and \(|1\rangle\) lies \(|\psi\rangle = \alpha|0\rangle + \beta|1\rangle\), but this is a superposition in a 2-dimensional space, not a point on a 1-dimensional continuum.

### 3.2 Morphisms

Morphisms in \(\mathcal{C}\) are **quantum gates** — unitary operators from a universal gate set \(\mathcal{G} = \{H, T, \text{CNOT}\}\) (or any equivalent universal set). Every morphism is a finite composition:

\[
U = U_K \circ U_{K-1} \circ ... \circ U_1
\]

where each \(U_i\) acts on 1 or 2 qubits from the set \(\{1, ..., n\}\).

**Key constraint:** Every morphism has a **finite description length** — it is specified by a finite string of symbols from a finite alphabet (gate labels + qubit indices).

### 3.3 What \(\mathcal{C}\) Cannot Represent

1. **Infinite-dimensional spaces:** No object in \(\mathcal{C}\) has dimension \(> 2^n\) for finite \(n\)
2. **Continuous coordinates:** No morphism can represent a smooth flow \(t \mapsto U(t)\) for \(t \in \mathbb{R}\) without discretization
3. **Symplectic structure:** No object carries a symplectic form — the Fubini-Study metric on \(\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{H}_2^{\otimes n})\) exists but is not the same object as the classical \(\omega\)
4. **Transcendental functions:** All gate entries are algebraic numbers (from the Clifford+T group or similar finite gate sets)

## 4. The Translation Functor \(F: \mathcal{P} \to \mathcal{C}\)

### 4.1 Object Mapping

\[
F: (M^{2n}, \omega) \mapsto \mathcal{H}_2^{\otimes N}
\]

where \(N \approx \log_2(\text{Vol}(M)/\hbar^n)\) is the number of qubits needed to "cover" the phase-space volume with resolution \(\hbar\). This is the **geometric quantization** of \(M\) truncated to a finite-dimensional subalgebra.

For the transmon: \(N = 1\) qubit (the forced 2-level projection). The full bosonic ladder has ~12 accessible levels, which would require \(N = \lceil\log_2(12)\rceil = 4\) qubits to represent faithfully — if we bothered to encode them all.

### 4.2 Morphism Mapping

\[
F: \varphi_t^H \mapsto U_{\text{dig}}(t) = \prod_{k=1}^{K} e^{-iH_k \Delta t_k/\hbar}
\]

where the continuous flow \(\varphi_t^H\) is replaced by a sequence of \(K\) discrete gates, each approximating the evolution over a short time step \(\Delta t_k\) using a Trotter-Suzuki decomposition. The mapping involves:
- **Temporal discretization:** \(t \mapsto \{t_1, t_2, ..., t_K\}\)
- **Spectral truncation:** \(H|_{\mathcal{H}_{\text{full}}} \mapsto H|_{\text{span}\{|0\rangle,|1\rangle\}}\)
- **Gate decomposition:** \(e^{-iH\Delta t} \mapsto \prod_j e^{-iH_j \Delta t}\) (Trotter)

## 5. Gromov's Non-Squeezing Theorem

### 5.1 Statement

(Gromov, 1985) Let \((M^{2n}, \omega = \sum dp_i \wedge dq_i)\) be the standard symplectic vector space. Define:
- The **ball**: \(B^{2n}(r) = \{(q,p) : \sum(q_i^2 + p_i^2) < r^2\}\)
- The **cylinder**: \(Z^{2n}(R) = \{(q,p) : q_1^2 + p_1^2 < R^2\}\)

If there exists a symplectic embedding \(\psi: B^{2n}(r) \hookrightarrow Z^{2n}(R)\), then **necessarily \(r \leq R\)**.

**Physical interpretation:** You cannot "squeeze" a symplectic ball through a narrow cylinder — the symplectic area in the \((q_1, p_1)\)-plane is a fundamental invariant. No Hamiltonian flow can compress it.

### 5.2 The Symplectic Capacity

Define the **Gromov width** of a symplectic manifold:

\[
c_G(M, \omega) = \sup \{ \pi r^2 : \text{there exists a symplectic embedding } B^{2n}(r) \hookrightarrow M \}
\]

This is a symplectic capacity — a numerical invariant that measures the "size" of the largest ball that fits inside \(M\). Symplectic capacities satisfy:

1. **Monotonicity:** If \(M_1 \hookrightarrow M_2\) symplectically, then \(c(M_1) \leq c(M_2)\)
2. **Conformality:** \(c(M, \lambda\omega) = \lambda \cdot c(M, \omega)\)
3. **Non-triviality:** \(c(B^{2n}(r)) = c(Z^{2n}(r)) = \pi r^2\)

## 6. Proof of Non-Exactness

### 6.1 Main Theorem

**Theorem 1 (Non-Exactness of \(F\)).** The translation functor \(F: \mathcal{P} \to \mathcal{C}\) is not exact — there exist objects and morphisms in \(\mathcal{P}\) whose images under \(F\) lose information that is present in the source.

**Proof.**

1. In \(\mathcal{P}\), the Gromov width \(c_G(M, \omega)\) is a well-defined invariant for any symplectic manifold. For the transmon: \(c_G(S^1 \times \mathbb{R}, d\phi \wedge dn) = \pi \hbar\) (the uncertainty cell area).

2. Any symplectic embedding preserves this invariant by non-squeezing: if \(\psi: M_1 \hookrightarrow M_2\), then \(c_G(M_1) \leq c_G(M_2)\).

3. In \(\mathcal{C}\), objects are finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces with no distinguished symplectic structure. The "volume" of a 2-dimensional submanifold of \(\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{H}_2^{\otimes n})\) is NOT a symplectic capacity — it is a Fubini-Study volume that depends on the embedding dimension and is not invariant under all unitary operations.

4. Specifically: Consider a symplectic ball \(B^{2n}(r) \in \mathcal{P}\) with Gromov width \(\pi r^2\). Under \(F\), this maps to a discrete point set — the computational basis states \(\{|0...0\rangle, ..., |1...1\rangle\}\) — which has **zero symplectic capacity** because there is no continuous structure to carry the volume.

5. Therefore: \(c_G(B^{2n}(r)) = \pi r^2\) while \(c_G(F(B^{2n}(r)))\) is undefined (or zero under any reasonable extension) — an invariant in \(\mathcal{P}\) is NOT preserved by \(F\).

6. If \(F\) were exact, any invariant computed in \(\mathcal{P}\) would have a corresponding "image invariant" in \(\mathcal{C}\) that is preserved under the functor. The absence of such an image invariant for the symplectic capacity proves non-exactness.

\(\square\)

### 6.2 Physical Corollary

The information lost under \(F\) is not a technical limitation — it is a **mathematical theorem.** No clever engineering, no better gates, no larger Hilbert space can make \(F\) exact. The symplectic capacity is a topological invariant of the physical system; the discrete gate model has no object that can carry it.

**For the transmon:** The 12-level bosonic ladder has a symplectic area of \(12\hbar\) in the \((\phi, n)\)-plane. The forced 2-level projection collapses this to zero. The "lost" area \(10\hbar\) is the physical manifestation of the non-exactness — it appears as leakage, DRAG overhead, and the entropy cost \(S_A\).

## 7. The Functor's Failure Modes

### 7.1 Three Types of Information Loss

| Failure Mode | Mathematical Origin | Physical Manifestation |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Spectral truncation** | \(\dim(F(\mathcal{H}_{\text{full}})) \ll \dim(\mathcal{H}_{\text{full}})\) | Leakage to higher transmon levels |
| **Temporal discretization** | \(t \in \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0} \mapsto \{t_1, ..., t_K\}\) | Trotter error (see Axis 4) |
| **Symplectic collapse** | \(\omega \mapsto \varnothing\) (no image) | Heisenberg violation at classical-quantum interface |

### 7.2 The de Gosson Connection

Maurice de Gosson (2012, arXiv:1203.5310) frames the same conflict as "The Angel of Geometry vs. the Demon of Algebra" — arguing that Gromov's non-squeezing imposes fundamental limits on quantum information processing because the symplectic capacity of quantum states resists compression.

**Our contribution beyond de Gosson:** We formalize this as an explicit **categorical non-exactness**, which:
1. Is a theorem, not a conjecture — follows directly from Gromov
2. Generalizes to any functor \(F: \mathcal{P} \to \mathcal{C}\) regardless of specific gate set or encoding scheme
3. Quantifies the lost information (see Axis 3 for \(S_A\)) rather than merely asserting its existence

## 8. Open Questions

1. **Is there ANY subcategory of \(\mathcal{P}\) where \(F\) is exact?** Possibly: completely integrable systems with action-angle coordinates where the action spectrum is discrete. Fermionic systems (spins) may admit exact functors because their phase space IS naturally discrete.

2. **Can we define a "better" functor \(F'\)?** If we expand \(\mathcal{C}\) to include continuous-variable quantum computation (infinite-dimensional Hilbert spaces, Gaussian operations), the symplectic capacity MAY have an image. This is the continuous-variable alternative to the discrete gate model.

3. **What is the minimal information loss?** Even if \(F\) is non-exact, can we bound the distance between \(F(\varphi)\) and the "true" image? This is precisely the Approximation Entropy \(S_A\) (Axis 3).
