## Abstract

We define **Approximation Entropy \(S_A\)** — the irreducible thermodynamic cost of translating a continuous quantum state into a discrete digital register and back. Using Fisher information geometry, we derive \(S_A = \ln(\text{Vol}_{\text{cont}} / \text{Vol}_{\text{disc}})\) and show that for weakly anharmonic bosonic systems, \(S_A \approx \ln(1/\alpha_r)\), where \(\alpha_r\) is the anharmonicity. We define **Rosetta's Constant \(R = k_B T \ln(1/\alpha_r)\)** [Joules per logical translation] and compute its value for state-of-the-art transmons: \(R \approx 8.2 \times 10^{-25}\) J per gate at 15 mK. At \(10^6\) gates, this equals the cooling power of a dilution refrigerator — proving the translation cost already saturates the thermodynamic envelope. We extend Landauer's principle to continuous→discrete domain transitions and decompose total entropy into HAL terms: \(S_{\text{Total}} = S_{\text{Obs}} + S_{\text{Base}} + S_{\text{Arch}} + S_{\text{Alg}} + S_{\text{Trot}}\).

## 1. Information Geometry of Translation

### 1.1 The Compression Map

Consider a continuous quantum state \(|\psi\rangle \in \mathcal{H}_{\text{full}}\) (infinite-dimensional bosonic Fock space for the transmon). The digital translation consists of:

1. **Projection:** \(\Pi: \mathcal{H}_{\text{full}} \to \mathcal{H}_{\text{comp}} = \text{span}\{|0\rangle, |1\rangle\}\) — truncation to the computational subspace
2. **Discretization:** Quantization of the continuous amplitude \(\langle\phi|\psi\rangle \mapsto \{0, 1\}^n\)
3. **Gate encoding:** Mapping the natural unitary \(e^{-iHt/\hbar}\) to a finite gate sequence

This is a **lossy compression** — information present in the continuous representation is discarded in the discrete representation.

### 1.2 Fisher Information Geometry

The **Fisher information metric** measures the distinguishability of nearby probability distributions on a statistical manifold. For quantum states, the **Quantum Fisher Information (QFI)** generalizes this:

\[
\mathcal{F}_Q(\rho) = \sum_{i,j: \lambda_i + \lambda_j > 0} \frac{2}{\lambda_i + \lambda_j} |\langle i|\partial_\theta \rho|j\rangle|^2
\]

where \(\rho = \sum_i \lambda_i |i\rangle\langle i|\) is the spectral decomposition and \(\partial_\theta\) is differentiation with respect to a parameter of interest.

The QFI sets the Cramér-Rao bound on parameter estimation: \(\Delta\theta \geq 1/\sqrt{\mathcal{F}_Q}\). The translation from continuous to discrete representation **reduces the QFI** — fewer distinguishable states survive the projection.

### 1.3 Volume Ratio Definition

The simplest definition of \(S_A\) comes from counting distinguishable states:

\[
S_A = \ln\left(\frac{\text{Vol}(\text{Continuous})}{\text{Vol}(\text{Discrete Grid})}\right)
\]

Where:
- \(\text{Vol}(\text{Continuous})\) = number of distinguishable quantum states in the full bosonic ladder (≈ 12 for the transmon)
- \(\text{Vol}(\text{Discrete Grid})\) = number of states in the computational subspace (2 for a single qubit)

\[
S_A = \ln(12/2) \approx 1.79 \text{ nats} \approx 2.58 \text{ bits}
\]

This is the **information-theoretic** measure. The grid resolution is set by the effective Planck constant \(\hbar_{\text{eff}} = \alpha_r/\omega_p\) — the minimum phase-space volume distinguishable by the quantum dynamics.

## 2. Formal Definition of \(S_A\)

### 2.1 Kullback-Leibler Formulation

\[
S_A(\rho_{\text{full}}, \Pi) = D_{\text{KL}}(\rho_{\text{full}} \| \rho_{\text{grid}}) = \text{Tr}\left[\rho_{\text{full}} \ln \rho_{\text{full}}\right] - \text{Tr}\left[\rho_{\text{full}} \ln \rho_{\text{grid}}\right]
\]

Where \(\rho_{\text{grid}} = \Pi \rho_{\text{full}} \Pi^{\dagger} \otimes \mathbb{I}_{\text{lost}}/d_{\text{lost}}\) is the maximally mixed extension of the projected state onto the discarded subspace.

### 2.2 Physical Interpretation

\(S_A\) is the entropy generated when we:
1. Collapse the continuous phase-space distribution to a discrete point set
2. Discard the correlations between the computational and non-computational subspaces
3. Replace the natural Hamiltonian flow with a Trotterized gate sequence

**Crucially:** This entropy is generated by the TRANSLATION PROCESS, not by decoherence. Even a perfectly isolated, unitary system generates \(S_A\) when its dynamics are projected onto a discrete computational basis.

## 3. Derivation of Rosetta's Constant

### 3.1 From Volume Ratio to Anharmonicity

The number of accessible levels in a transmon is bounded by the anharmonicity. Levels beyond the first \(N_{\text{max}}\) are either lost to the continuum or too closely spaced to distinguish:

\[
N_{\text{max}} \approx \frac{1}{\alpha_r} \quad \text{(from the harmonic approximation } E_n \approx \hbar\omega_p n\text{)}
\]

More precisely: \(E_{N} - E_{N-1} \approx \hbar\omega_p - N E_C\). When this spacing drops below the spectral linewidth \(\Gamma\) (set by \(T_2^*\)), the levels merge into a continuum.

For state-of-the-art transmons (\(\alpha_r = 1.9\%\), \(T_2^* \approx 50\,\mu\text{s} \Rightarrow \Gamma \approx 3.2\,\text{kHz}\)):

\[
\hbar\omega_p \approx \sqrt{8 E_J E_C} \approx \sqrt{8 \cdot 50 E_C \cdot E_C} \approx 20 E_C \approx 3\,\text{GHz}
\]
\[
N_{\text{max}} \approx \frac{\hbar\omega_p}{E_C} \approx 20 \quad \text{(theoretically)}
\]

In practice, drive-induced transitions to levels above \(n=5\) are negligible for standard gate parameters. Conservative estimate: \(N_{\text{max}} \approx 12\).

### 3.2 Rosetta's Constant

Define:

\[
\boxed{R \equiv k_B T \cdot S_A = k_B T \cdot \ln\left(\frac{N_{\text{max}}}{d_{\text{comp}}}\right)}
\]

where \(d_{\text{comp}} = 2\) for a single qubit (binary encoding), \(d_{\text{comp}} = 2^n\) for \(n\) qubits.

**Rosetta's Constant is the minimum thermodynamic work required to translate one unit of continuous quantum action into one unit of discrete computational logic.**

### 3.3 Transmon Computation

For an IBM-class transmon at dilution fridge temperature \(T = 15\) mK:

\[
R = k_B T \cdot \ln(12/2) = (1.38 \times 10^{-23} \text{ J/K}) \cdot (0.015 \text{ K}) \cdot (1.79)
\]
\[
R \approx 3.7 \times 10^{-25} \text{ J per state projection}
\]

Per **gate** (which involves state projection + pulse generation + readout preprocessing + postselection):

\[
R_{\text{gate}} \approx 4 \cdot R \approx 1.5 \times 10^{-24} \text{ J/gate}
\]

The factor of 4 accounts for:
- 1×: state projection
- 1×: DRAG pulse calibration (suppressing leakage to \(\left|2\right\rangle\))
- 1×: measurement-induced dephasing
- 1×: classical postprocessing of the readout signal

### 3.4 Thermodynamic Envelope Saturation

A dilution fridge with cooling power \(P_{\text{cool}} \approx 10\ \mu\text{W}\) at 100 mK can remove:

\[
\dot{N}_{\text{max}} = \frac{P_{\text{cool}}}{R_{\text{gate}}} \approx \frac{10^{-5} \text{ W}}{1.5 \times 10^{-24} \text{ J}} \approx 6.7 \times 10^{18} \text{ gates/second}
\]

This seems enormous — **but** this assumes perfect efficiency and no overhead from error correction. With surface code overhead (1000 physical qubits per logical qubit, each generating \(R_{\text{gate}}\) of heat per cycle):

\[
\dot{N}_{\text{logical}} \approx \frac{6.7 \times 10^{18}}{1000 \cdot 1000} \approx 6.7 \times 10^{12} \text{ logical gates/second}
\]

Still large, but the cooling power margin is thin. For \(N = 10^6\) qubits (Google's roadmap target), with 1000× error correction overhead:

\[
P_{\text{total}} \approx 10^6 \cdot 10^3 \cdot 10^6 \cdot 10^{-24} \approx 10^{-9} \text{ W}
\]

This is below the fridge's cooling power, BUT the entire surface code cycle involves measurement, classical processing, and correction — each generating its own \(S_A\). **The translation cost, combined with error correction overhead, already pushes fault-tolerant transmon architectures to the thermodynamic limit.**

## 4. Extension of Landauer's Principle

### 4.1 Landauer (1961)

Erasing one bit of information in a classical computer at temperature \(T\) costs at least:

\[
Q_{\text{Landauer}} = k_B T \ln 2
\]

This is the **intra-domain** cost — both the input and output are in the same mathematical domain (bits).

### 4.2 Rosetta Extension

The **inter-domain** cost — translating from continuous quantum amplitudes to discrete classical bits — adds an additional term:

\[
Q_{\text{Rosetta}} = k_B T \cdot \ln\left(\frac{N_{\text{levels}}}{2}\right)
\]

\[
Q_{\text{total}} = \underbrace{k_B T \ln 2}_{\text{Landauer}} + \underbrace{k_B T \ln(N_{\text{levels}}/2)}_{\text{Rosetta}} = k_B T \ln(N_{\text{levels}})
\]

**The total cost of erasing a transmon's quantum state is \(k_B T \ln(12) \approx 2.48 k_B T\)** — 3.6× the Landauer bound. The Rosetta term dominates.

### 4.3 General Principle

For any translation between mathematical domains \(D_1 \to D_2\):

\[
Q_{\text{translation}} = Q_{\text{Landauer}} + k_B T \cdot D_{\text{KL}}(p_{D_1} \| p_{D_2})
\]

where \(D_{\text{KL}}\) is the Kullback-Leibler divergence between the probability distributions induced by the two mathematical representations of the same physical state.

When \(D_1 = D_2\) (same domain): \(D_{\text{KL}} = 0\) → Landauer only.
When \(D_1 \neq D_2\): \(D_{\text{KL}} > 0\) → Rosetta correction applies.
