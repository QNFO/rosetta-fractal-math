## Quick Start

This project formalizes and quantifies the irreducible cost of translating physical dynamics from their native continuous mathematical frameworks (differential geometry, functional analysis) into discrete digital computational logic (Boolean algebra, quantum circuits). We call this cost **Approximation Entropy (S_A)**.

The primary case study is the superconducting transmon qubit — a bosonic analog oscillator forced into a discrete digital paradigm. But the thesis extends to any domain where continuous natural dynamics are projected onto discrete human measurement/cognition frameworks.

**Core claim:** Digital universality is a subset, not a superset, of physical computability. The translation cost S_A scales as ln(1/α_r) for weakly anharmonic systems, making digital fault-tolerant quantum computing exponentially more expensive than analog simulation for bosonic platforms.
